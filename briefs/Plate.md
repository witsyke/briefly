## Summary

This paper formulates optimal experimental design (OED) as a mixed-integer optimal control problem for universal differential equations (UDE) — hybrid models that embed a neural network inside a first-principles ODE. The core contribution addresses a scaling problem: with many ANN weights, the sensitivity equations and Fisher information matrix (FIM) become huge and singular (structural non-identifiability), making the OED problem intractable. The authors propose and compare dimension-reduction strategies — truncated SVD of the FIM versus a novel "lumping" approach that introduces artificial scaling parameters (per-network, or per-layer) multiplied onto groups of weights — to obtain low-dimensional, well-posed variational ODEs whose FIM is invertible. They also extend a "global information gain" criterion (derived from Pontryagin's maximum principle) to explain and choose the SVD truncation rank, showing convergence of optimal sampling decisions as more singular values/components are retained. Benchmarks on a Lotka-Volterra predator-prey UDE and a urethane chemical-reaction UDE show that jointly optimizing control excitation and binary sampling decisions (when to measure, under a fixed measurement budget) substantially reduces parameter/weight uncertainty and improves extrapolation compared to equidistant sampling.

## Takeaways

- Directly relevant OED formulation: the (OED) mixed-integer optimal control problem here treats "when to measure" as binary control variables `w(t)` with hard budget constraints `z_i(t_f) ≤ M_i` — essentially the "choose the next time point under resource constraints" problem this project targets, formalized via sensitivity ODEs (VDE) and the Fisher information matrix, with A/D/E-optimality criteria.
- The "global information gain" concept (Π^i(t) = F⁻¹ P^i(t) F⁻¹, from Pontryagin's maximum principle) gives a principled, a posteriori way to interpret which time intervals are information-rich and to relate Lagrange multipliers on the budget constraint to per-time-point value of an additional measurement — could be adapted as a stopping/prioritization criterion for sequential/greedy time-point selection instead of only solving the full binary optimal control problem.
- The dimension-reduction machinery (SVD-truncation of the FIM, and cheaper "lumping" via artificial scaling parameters) is a candidate technique whenever your model has many nuisance/high-dimensional parameters (e.g., a flexible spatial or temporal component) that would otherwise make the FIM singular and the design problem ill-posed — the lumping approach avoids costly repeated SVDs and is proposed as a fast heuristic alternative.
- Design is purely temporal (no spatial dimension) and demonstrated only on engineering ODE benchmarks (Lotka-Volterra, a chemical reaction), not on spatio-temporal or epidemiological data — epidemiology (COVID-19 regional transmission UDEs) is only cited as related work, not applied here, so the spatial/location-choice extension for this project's use case would need to be developed independently.
- Practical implementation notes worth reusing: relaxing binary sampling decisions to [0,1] and solving with a local NLP solver (Ipopt) exploits a proven bang-bang property of optimal continuous-time sampling solutions, and the paper's open-source Julia tooling (`DynamicOED.jl`) is a concrete reference implementation for FIM-based OED with control/sampling co-design.
